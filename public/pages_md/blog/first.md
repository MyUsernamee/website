### Nice, but why are you doing this again?
Outside of that, I kind of want to discuss why I am making this blog site. A thing I struggled with for a long time is that I personally hate attention-seeking. I don't like attention if that makes sense. I don't like it when people do things for attention, almost narcissistically. So, for me, making something like a blog site or YouTube didn't make much sense. Recently, though, I've found that obviously there are other reasons to make a blog or portfolio. For one job prospect. As I progress my way through college, I've learned that internships are extremely valuable in this field. So, as a result, I need to try to get one. One way to really put your best foot forward is with side projects. What better way to present them than with a website? Another reason, and this one I think works for me more, is for it to almost act like a journal. I've never been one to journal, mostly because I forget, but I've heard it is a really great way to track progress and continue progression on projects, goals, etc. This makes making a blog a really good resource for this. One more reason is that it gives you another source of motivation. If I start a project, make a post, and then forget about it, it almost feels like I've given up on an obligation. Those last two were probably the most important for me. Like I mentioned, I am terrible at finishing projects, and it is something I still really struggle with; So as another means to try and fix that, this came about.

### Setup

Lastly, would I really be a programmer if I didn't talk about my setup? I originally started my computing experience with Windows, which, if I am going to be honest, I hated every step of the way. It just continually managed to disappoint and went through the craziest ride of **enshitification**. So at some point, as soon as I could, I switched over to Linux. For the longest time, I ran mostly Ubuntu. It had lots of support and was relatively easy to get into. I ran KDE Plasma on it for quite some time, then I went back to Windows for a bit. This was mostly because support for some software that I needed still wasn't there, and I just got tired of switching back and forth. Then, not many weeks after using Windows again, I remembered why I left. It was terrible. So I got a new SSD and put the latest version of Ubuntu on there, 23.10 (Why I didn't choose LTS, I don't know). Using this for a while was good, the new GNOME look was great, and I was loving it. It was simple, relatively lightweight, and just did what I needed it to do, no more, no less. Of course, at some point I realized 23.10 wasn't probably the best pick because of incompatibility and other things. Not long after I installed that, though, 24.04 came out. I did a dist-upgrade, and it was smooth sailing from there. The most issues I ever encountered were... Honestly, I can't really think of anything. Maybe Assetto Corsa doesn't work without a little bit of time, which I wasn't willing to put in. I mean, even VR worked now. I was able to use my quest with any game I wanted. Then my Spring 2025 term started. I got an M1 MacBook Air. Honestly, the default experience wasn't terrible. Having a Unix-like system was really nice, and things like Homebrew made the experience even better, but not having the ability to just nuke my desktop environment if I wanted to was bothering me. I loved the battery life and everything else outside of that. Throughout this term, this is all I ran. At some point before this semester, I started using Vimium in VSCode, but I really wanted to use just NeoVim. I had trouble setting it up before, and some of the extensions I needed weren't there (Of course, they were, I just didn't know how to set anything up at the time). Eventually, on my Mac though, I was tired of VSCode being VSCode and I just said heck it, nvim. I looked up some tutorials to get it working with Rust as a bare minimum and found NVChad. It was great. I didn't have to do a lot of long setup, and a lot of things worked out of the box. I used this knowledge alone and plopped it on my main computer and haven't touched VSCode since. In my spring term, I used nvim with LaTeX to take math notes. This didn't actually help any, cause the Calculus 2 teacher was terrible. I think out of the 30-40 students in that class, only like 2-3 actually passed. So all of us retook it in the summer, and my entire class was there for the summer term lmao. During this time, I knew of Linux on Mac through Asahi, but I heard that its support wasn't great. Before installing it, I at some point heard of Hyprland, and I tried to get it running on Ubuntu, but I just didn't want to invest the time to get it working. I then found out about Asahi and how it actually had proper GPU support and had really come a long way. So I decided to dual-boot it. By default, it came with Fedora, which had Hyprland as a package. It actually had a lot of software as packages, and generally was more stable. I got started with Hyprland and took the catppuccin style from Typecraft. It was amazing. So good in fact that, with the new NVME SSD my friend Meetric sent me, THANK YOU AGAIN, I installed Fedora and synced my Hyprland configs. This is where I am today. I use Neovim with modified NVChad. I use Hyprland with Fedora, and I used Vim-latex to take math notes ;).
