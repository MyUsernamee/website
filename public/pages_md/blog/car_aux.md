# Fixing my Car’s Aux
#### I fixed my car’s aux port and gave it Bluetooth.

Hello again! So, I want to talk about a really simple project I did over the course of a day. So I got a 2009 Toyota Camry . This car sports needed features, plus good fuel usage, despite being basic. So far, I’ve really enjoyed having it. I’ve done quite some miles, and outside of the occasionally easy-to-fix electrical issues, I’ve had no complaints. Except for one thing. I love my music. I hate driving minus tunes. Of course, there are some good stations, like Old School 104.7, but sometimes the songs just are not hitting the way they need to. Ideally, I could use Spotify. Sadly, the aux port in my car didn’t work for some odd reason. At some point, I found out the cable had to be held a certain way while in the port for it to work. Looking this problem up on Google, apparently it was fairly common; there is even a [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uFXBJBtXXe0) on how to fix this issue. So after giving it a shot, the port sadly still didn’t work. The actual jack was just dead. So instead, I decided to just bake the Bluetooth right in! I had enough random spare parts around to just smack it on.

## The Chinese Bluetooth adapter.

So, at some point, I got a Bluetooth to aux adapter for my sister for her car. This adapter at some point, though, broke. The connector broke, and it was acting weird. So I had just gotten my hands on an ESP-32, and I thought I could just make my own. So I did, and I will have to write a post about that as well. Anyway, I was left with this little adapter. Opening it up revealed a little lithium pillow (pleasant surprise) and a tiny little PCB. I quickly removed the angry pillow and put the PCB in my little spare-part stash. Fast forward to now, and I have a PCB that goes to my car, but not an aux port to replace the broken one. There was an aux port on this PCB, but I thought I should just skip that part since I am probably going to use this little Bluetooth thing anyway and just solder it directly to the aux port PCB. So after I messed it up three times somehow and soldered a copper wire the width of my hair to trick the thing into thinking a cable was always plugged in, it was ready for testing. After hearing the “Power on” in that classic cheap Chinese Bluetooth device voice, I hear my music!!! Out of only one speaker :(. Sadly the left channel coming out of the PCB was just blank. Running a continuity test on the two channels, they were tied together; no idea why. Also, this little IC for the Bluetooth didn’t save states between power-offs, so I have to repair it every time I restart my car (That is probably why they included a lithium battery, but I don’t see why they couldn’t just use flash???). Now I can enjoy my music of somewhat okay quality. Next, I want to build another Bluetooth receiver with another ESP-32 I have, but I need to get a nice DAC for that or build one ;).
