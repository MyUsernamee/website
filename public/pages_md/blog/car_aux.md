# Fixing my Car's Aux
#### I fix my car's aux port and give it bluetooh.

Hello again! So I want to talk about a really simple project I did over the course of a day. So I got a Toyota Camry 2009. It is a really simple car but it has all the features I need and it is fuel efficent enough. So far, I've really enjoyed having it. I've done quite some miles, and outside of the occasionally easy to fix electrical issues, I've had no complaints. That is except one thing. I love my music. Not being able to listen to some good music while I am driving is driving me mad. Of course there are some good stations, like old school 104.7, but sometimes the songs just are not hitting the way they need too. Ideally I would be able to use spotify. Sadly the aux port in my car didn't work for some odd reason, at some point I found out the cable had to be held a certain way while in the port for it work. Looking this problem up on good appearently it was fairly common there is even a [video]() on how to fix this issue. So after giving it a shot the port sadly still didn't work. The actual jack was just dead. So instead I decided to just bake the bluetooth right in! I had enough random spare parts around to just smack it on.

## The chinese bluetooth adapter.

So, at some point I got a bluetooth to aux adapter for my sister for her car. This adapter at somepoint though, broke. The connector broke and it was acting werid. So at the time I had just got my hand on an ESP-32, and I thought, I could just make my own. So I did and I will have to write a post on that as well. Anyways, I was left with this little adapter. Opening it up reveiled a little lithium pillow (good suprise) and a tiny little pcb. I quickly removed the angry pillow and put the pcb in my little spare part stash. Fast forward to now and I have a pcb that goes to my car but not a aux port to replace the broken one. There was a aux port on this pcb, but I thought I should just skip that part since I am probably going to use this little bluetooth thing anyways and just solder it directly to the aux port pcb. So after messing it up three times somehow and soldering a copper wire the width of my hair to trick it to thinking a cable was always plugged in, it was ready to test. After hearing the "Power on" in that classic cheap chinese bluetooh device voice, I hear my music!!! Out of only one speaker :(. Sadly for some reason the left channel coming out of the pcb was just blank. Running a continuity test on the two channels they were tied together for some reason, no idea. Also this little IC for the bluetooth didn't save states between power offs, so I have to repair every time I restart my car (That is probably why they included a lithium battery, but I don't see why they couldn't just use flash???). Now I can enjoy my music in somewhat okay quality. Next I want to build another bluetooth receiver with another ESP-32 I have but I need to get a nice DAC for that or build one ;).
